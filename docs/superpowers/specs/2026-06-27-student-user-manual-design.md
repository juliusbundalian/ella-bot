# E.L.L.A. Bilingual Student User Manual Design

**Date:** 2026-06-27  
**Status:** Approved in conversation  
**Audience:** Philippine ARAL Program learners from Kindergarten to Grade 10  

## Goal

Create two coordinated, student-facing materials for E.L.L.A.:

1. A 10–12 page illustrated bilingual user manual in editable Word and print-ready PDF formats.
2. A one-page bilingual quick-start guide in editable Word and print-ready PDF formats.

The materials must help ARAL learners use E.L.L.A. confidently with minimal text. Older learners should be able to use the materials independently, while younger and emerging readers should be able to follow them with a tutor's assistance.

## Program and Product Context

The Academic Recovery and Accessible Learning (ARAL) Program serves learners from Kindergarten to Grade 10 who need support reaching grade-level competencies. ARAL-Reading prioritizes learners who are below expected reading proficiency. The materials must therefore be learner-centered, encouraging, and usable across a wide range of ages and reading abilities.

E.L.L.A. is an offline English reading assistant. It presents a target sound, word, phrase, or sentence; speaks the target; listens to the learner read aloud; gives spoken coaching; records attempts; and presents completion results.

Authoritative ARAL references:

- [Republic Act No. 12028, Academic Recovery and Accessible Learning Program Act](https://lawphil.net/statutes/repacts/ra2024/ra_12028_2024.html)
- [DepEd launch of ARAL-Reading for School Year 2025–2026](https://www.deped.gov.ph/2025/09/13/deped-naglunsad-ng-aral-program-para-palakasin-ang-pagbasa-at-pagkatuto/)

## Audience Model

The same manual serves all grade groups. There will be no separate grade-band manuals.

- **Kindergarten to Grade 3:** primarily picture-led and tutor-assisted.
- **Grades 4 to 6:** picture-led with short instructions and occasional tutor support.
- **Grades 7 to 10:** independently usable, without childish or stigmatizing wording.
- **Reading profile:** may include emerging and frustrated readers; the manual must not assume fluent reading.
- **Language:** bilingual English–Filipino.

## Deliverables

### Full illustrated manual

- A4 portrait.
- Approximately 10–12 pages, excluding an optional blank back cover.
- Editable `.docx` source.
- Print-ready `.pdf` export.
- Uses current E.L.L.A. screenshots captured from the approved release build.

### One-page quick-start guide

- One A4 page.
- Editable `.docx` source.
- Print-ready `.pdf` export.
- Uses the same six-step sequence, terms, colors, icons, and screenshots as the full manual.
- Suitable for posting beside the E.L.L.A. device or giving directly to a learner.

## Language and Tone

### Bilingual pattern

Each instructional block uses this order:

1. Short English instruction.
2. Natural Filipino translation immediately below it.
3. The same step number, icon, or screenshot callout for both languages.

English and Filipino will not be separated into different editions or distant columns. Keeping each translation pair together reduces searching and helps tutors point to one instruction at a time.

Visible interface labels remain exactly as they appear in E.L.L.A. The Filipino text refers to those labels without translating the button itself. Example:

> Tap **Start**.  
> Pindutin ang **Start**.

### Writing rules

- Use active voice and direct instructions.
- Give one action per sentence.
- Prefer sentences of 10 words or fewer when the meaning remains clear.
- Use familiar Filipino rather than formal or literal translation.
- Avoid technical terms such as ASR, TTS, WER, model, backend, or session log.
- Explain the word *fluency* once as smooth and accurate reading.
- Describe a retry as another chance to practice, never as punishment.
- Use calm, supportive wording for errors and low scores.
- Use **learner** or **student**, not labels that define a child by a reading difficulty.

## Information Architecture

The full manual follows the learner's actual journey through the application.

### Page 1: Cover

- Title: **Using E.L.L.A. / Paggamit ng E.L.L.A.**
- Subtitle: **Bilingual Student User Manual / Bilingual na Gabay para sa Mag-aaral**
- E.L.L.A. character artwork.
- Manual version and application revision in small footer text.

### Page 2: Meet E.L.L.A. and get ready

- Explain that E.L.L.A. listens to the learner read English aloud and gives practice feedback.
- Sit where the microphone can hear the learner.
- Reduce nearby noise.
- Face the device and speak in a clear, natural voice.
- Ask a tutor for help when needed.

### Page 3: The six-step reading activity

1. Tap **Start**.
2. Look at the sound, word, phrase, or sentence.
3. Listen while E.L.L.A. reads it.
4. Read it aloud when E.L.L.A. starts listening.
5. Wait while E.L.L.A. checks the reading.
6. Listen to the feedback, then retry or continue.

This page is the source content for the one-page quick-start guide.

### Page 4: Main Menu and Settings

- Identify **Start**, **Exit**, and the Settings gear.
- Explain that **Start** begins or resumes reading practice.
- Show how to return from Settings with **Back**.
- Keep **Exit** guidance brief and mention its confirmation dialog.

### Page 5: The reading screen

- Identify the current level and item number.
- Identify the target reading text.
- Identify the Options menu button.
- Explain E.L.L.A.'s speaking, listening, thinking/processing, success, and retry character states using icons and screenshots.
- Tell learners not to repeatedly tap the screen while E.L.L.A. is listening or checking.

### Page 6: Options during practice

- Open and close **Options**.
- Increase or decrease **Volume**.
- Adjust **Listening Time** from 5 to 10 seconds.
- Use **Restart Level** only when the learner intends to begin that level again.
- Use **Back to Menu** to leave the reading activity.
- Explain the Yes/No confirmation dialogs.

### Page 7: Listening to feedback and trying again

- Wait until E.L.L.A. finishes speaking.
- Follow the pronunciation hint or modeled reading.
- Read the same item again when prompted.
- Reassure learners that needing another attempt is a normal part of practice.
- Show the success and retry character states without relying on color alone.

### Page 8: Understanding the results screen

- **Score** means items read correctly on the first attempt.
- **Fluency** summarizes smooth and accurate reading across all attempts.
- **Rating** uses A, B, C, D, or F.
- A result of C or higher allows progression under the current application rules.
- **Continue** advances after a sub-level.
- **Next Level** advances after a full tier.
- **Retry** starts the applicable sub-level or tier again.
- **Main Menu** may ask whether to continue progress or restart from the beginning.

The page emphasizes improvement and practice rather than competition.

### Page 9: E.L.L.A.'s reading levels

- Level 1A–1G: letter sounds, sound combinations, and blends.
- Level 2A–2D: sight words and increasingly difficult words.
- Level 3: phrases.
- Level 4: full sentences and connected text.
- Explain that E.L.L.A. remembers completed progress and resumes from the next incomplete level when the application is reopened.

### Page 10: Settings and resetting progress

- Explain the six volume positions.
- Explain the 5–10 second listening-time range.
- Explain that **Reset Progress** returns the learner to Level 1A and removes the saved progress history used by E.L.L.A.
- Place a warning icon beside the reset instruction.
- Show the Yes/No reset confirmation.

### Page 11: Simple troubleshooting

Use a two-column problem/action pattern with no more than five cases:

- E.L.L.A. did not hear me → wait for listening, face the microphone, speak clearly, and reduce noise.
- I need more time → increase **Listening Time**.
- I cannot hear E.L.L.A. → increase **Volume** and ask the tutor to check the device.
- E.L.L.A. heard a different word → try once more from a comfortable distance and speak naturally.
- The screen does not continue or shows an error → stop tapping and ask the tutor for help.

### Page 12: Finishing and getting help

- Show **Play Again** and **Main Menu** on the final evaluation screen.
- Remind the learner that practice and improvement matter more than one rating.
- Provide a clearly marked **Ask Your Tutor / Humingi ng Tulong sa Tutor** box.
- End with an encouraging E.L.L.A. message.

## One-Page Quick-Start Layout

The quick-start guide uses a 2-by-3 grid of large numbered panels:

1. **Tap Start / Pindutin ang Start**
2. **Look / Tumingin**
3. **Listen / Makinig**
4. **Read aloud / Bumasa nang malakas**
5. **Wait / Maghintay**
6. **Retry or continue / Subukan muli o magpatuloy**

Each panel contains one icon, one cropped screenshot or character state, one English line, and one Filipino line. A footer contains two reminders:

- Use E.L.L.A. in a quiet place.
- Ask your tutor when you need help.

## Visual Design

- Use the existing E.L.L.A. pink palette and character artwork.
- Use large, high-contrast text suitable for A4 printing.
- Limit each page to one main task and three to five visible actions.
- Use numbered callouts rather than paragraphs to explain screenshots.
- Highlight controls with outlines and arrows; do not obscure the underlying label.
- Never use color as the only way to communicate state or meaning.
- Pair color with a word, icon, shape, or number.
- Maintain generous white space and a consistent reading order.
- Avoid decorative elements that compete with the target button or instruction.
- Use the same icon for the same action in both deliverables.

## Student Flow Covered

```text
Launch E.L.L.A.
    → introductory greeting
    → Main Menu
    → Start
    → target appears and E.L.L.A. speaks
    → E.L.L.A. listens
    → E.L.L.A. checks the reading
    → success or coaching feedback
    → next item or retry
    → sub-level/tier result
    → Continue, Next Level, Retry, or Main Menu
    → final evaluation
    → Play Again or Main Menu
```

The manual also documents the Settings path, in-practice Options path, confirmations, and safe recovery when the learner needs assistance.

## Source-of-Truth and Publication Gate

The current repository is suitable for defining the manual, but the materials must not be published from the existing screenshots without a release check.

Observed on 2026-06-27:

- The four repository screenshots were last modified on 2026-05-27; student-facing code changed afterward.
- `config/level_pools.json` has an uncommitted change that replaces most Level 1A vowels with an empty second item.
- `config/settings.ini` has uncommitted changes to listening time and fullscreen behavior.
- The system `pytest -q` command cannot collect `tests/test_tts_piper.py` because NumPy is missing from the system Python environment. This is not the correct project test command.
- The project environment command `.venv/bin/python -m pytest -q` reports 104 passing and 5 failing tests. Two failures concern modeled feedback and a single-letter homophone match. Three concern Piper speech configuration, explicit voice-model selection, and audio-stop behavior. All five can affect the learner experience or the accuracy of manual instructions.

Before screenshots or final copy are produced, the project owner must identify the approved release revision, confirm the Level 1A content and default settings, use the project virtual environment for verification, and resolve or explicitly accept the five failing tests.

## Production Workflow

1. Freeze and verify the release build.
2. Create a screenshot manifest containing the application revision, display resolution, state, and intended manual page for every image.
3. Capture and crop the screenshot matrix.
4. Draft concise English instructions against the actual interface.
5. Translate each block into natural Filipino while preserving English interface labels.
6. Build the full manual in Word using reusable paragraph, callout, warning, caption, and screenshot styles.
7. Build the one-page guide from the approved six-step content and the same visual assets.
8. Render both Word documents to PDF and inspect every page visually.
9. Verify every instruction by performing it on the approved build.
10. Print-test both PDFs in color and grayscale on A4 paper.
11. Conduct short usability checks with learners from Grades 1–3, 4–6, and 7–10, allowing tutor assistance for younger learners.
12. Apply only findings that improve task completion, comprehension, or accessibility; repeat rendering and functional verification after changes.
13. Record the final application revision and manual version in both documents.

## Usability Check

Each grade band should include at least two representative ARAL learners when feasible. A tutor may read the instruction aloud for learners who cannot yet read the manual independently, but the tutor should not demonstrate the correct control unless the learner is blocked.

Test these tasks:

1. Start a reading activity from the Main Menu.
2. Identify when to listen and when to read aloud.
3. Increase the listening time.
4. Return to the Main Menu without resetting all progress.
5. Explain what **Retry** means.
6. Identify the next action on a results screen.
7. Find the instruction for getting tutor help.

Record whether the learner completes each task independently, with one prompt, or only after demonstration. Revise any instruction that repeatedly requires demonstration.

## Acceptance Criteria

- Both deliverables are bilingual English–Filipino.
- There is one shared manual for all grades, not separate grade-band manuals.
- The full manual is 10–12 A4 pages and the quick-start guide is one A4 page.
- Both deliverables are supplied as editable Word files and print-ready PDFs.
- Every interface label matches the approved build exactly.
- Every screenshot comes from the approved release revision and is listed in the screenshot manifest.
- Each learner-facing instruction is paired with its Filipino translation.
- Critical actions use numbered visual steps.
- Resetting or restarting actions include visible warnings and confirmation guidance.
- Results terminology matches the application: Score, Fluency, Rating, Continue, Next Level, Retry, and Main Menu.
- The documents remain understandable when printed in grayscale.
- Functional verification confirms that every documented action works as stated.
- Visual inspection confirms that no text, image, callout, or page element is clipped or unreadable.
- Usability checks include learners from all three grade bands or document why a band could not be recruited before publication.

## Out of Scope

- Installing Python, E.L.L.A., speech models, or audio drivers.
- Configuring Raspberry Pi, ReSpeaker, Piper, Vosk, or command-line flags.
- Diagnosing hardware or operating-system failures.
- Explaining session-log files or technical scoring formulas.
- Providing a tutor, teacher, administrator, or developer manual.
- Producing separate manuals for different grade bands.

## Maintenance

Include the manual version, application revision, and publication date in each source and PDF. Retake affected screenshots and re-verify instructions whenever a student-facing label, control, screen layout, progression rule, scoring definition, or confirmation flow changes. Keep the screenshot manifest with the editable sources so future revisions can identify which pages require updates.
